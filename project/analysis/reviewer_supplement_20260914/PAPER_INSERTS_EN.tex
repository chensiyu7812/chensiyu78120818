% Abstract: add near the final sentence.
Development uses controlled synthetic states, and external evaluation uses
EvoEmo virtual users. The resource advantage is relative to the two specified
structured-resource baselines; the current evidence does not establish a
benefit over Context Only or a cost-matched fixed action.

% Methods: Rule Source Selector.
\paragraph{Rule Source Selector.}
The query concatenates the current-session summary, visible recent messages,
and current user message. The rule hashes word unigrams and bigrams into
64 nonnegative, L2-normalized dimensions and compares the query with each
available source's catalog fingerprint by cosine similarity. It selects up
to two sources above the frozen thresholds
$\theta_{MP}=\theta_{MS}=\theta_{ME}=0$.
Strategy RAG is enabled when the maximum lexical cosine similarity to a
Strategy Bank card is at least $0.08$. The rule uses catalog fingerprints;
the final stable PM uses dialogue and inventory metadata without these
fingerprints. On the 204 scored external states, the rule selects MS+ME+RS
in every case; this is a property of this sample, not a constant-policy
definition of the rule.

% Methods: inventory construction and validation.
\paragraph{Inventory variants and development units.}
Each of the 192 synthetic source states is expanded into nine inventory
variants: no memory, MP, MS, high-relevance ME, low-relevance ME, MP+MS,
MP+high-ME, MS+low-ME, and MP+MS+high-ME. High and low ME are selected by
lexical similarity to the same current context, with memory identifiers
breaking ties; both ME variants expose one item. Variant labels are
construction-only and are not model inputs. The resulting 1,728 cards
contain 192, 768, 576, and 192 cards with 2, 4, 8, and 16 legal actions,
respectively, yielding 11,136 state--action responses. The 16 users are
grouped into five folds, keeping all variants of a source state together.
User identities are held out; the 36 current-user utterances and 12 semantic
families are reused across folds. Thus, this is a controlled learnability
diagnostic rather than a test of unseen language families.

\paragraph{Development score and prediction scales.}
The development response-preference score is distinct from the external
1--5 Emotional Support rating. Within each card, pair labels are coded as
$y\in\{-1,0,1\}$ and projected onto action score differences using
$\hat z=(B^\top B+0.10I)^{-1}B^\top y$, where each row of $B$ contrasts two
actions. After centering $\hat z$, the diagnostic score of an action is its
mean $\sigma(\hat z_a-\hat z_b)$ against the other legal actions.
The deployed logistic ranker is trained on non-tie feature differences with
mirrored orientations and equal total weight per card. Its sigmoid-transformed
action score is an ordering score, not an independently calibrated probability
of successful support. The five ridge heads predict normalized annotation
targets and are clipped to $[0,1]$; they are not clinical risk estimates.

% Replace "calibrated risk constraints" with "thresholds on predicted
% annotation-based risk scores" unless separate calibration evidence exists.
\paragraph{Legality, feasibility, and fallback.}
Legal actions respect source availability. Feasible actions additionally
satisfy predicted-risk thresholds. The frozen setting is
$\epsilon=0$, $\tau_{mis}=0.35$, and $\tau_{omit}=\tau_{str}=1$.
Since predictions are bounded by one, the latter two thresholds do not
exclude actions. The highest predicted response score is selected within
the feasible set; exact ties are resolved by estimated cost, memory decision
quality, strategy decision quality, response score, and action identifier.
The cost proxy sums estimated top-$k$ memory tokens, 260 tokens if RS is
enabled, and a penalty of 24 per source invocation. It is not measured
end-to-end latency or total generator input. Confirmatory inference stops
with an error if no action is feasible. A least-violation fallback is
available only in explicitly enabled development searches, and settings
that use it are excluded from final validation selection.

% External setting: concrete adapter and unit definition.
\paragraph{External memory and fixed inputs.}
MP contains separate profile fields; MS contains historical session
summaries. Each external ME item concatenates the seeker messages from one
past session in temporal order; ME is not constructed from gold event
timelines. The generator receives the most recent eight prior messages and
the current seeker message, with an empty current-session summary in this
adapter. The 204 scored states comprise 34 scenarios, three fixed seeker
seeds, and turns 3 and 8, shared across policies. These seeker seeds are
not independent PM training seeds.

% Results: make the scope of the main comparison explicit.
\paragraph{Comparison with Context Only.}
The original six-candidate GPT-4o evaluation does not show an external
response benefit for structured resource injection over Context Only.
PM minus Context Only is $-0.088$ for Emotional Support and $-0.098$ for
Personalization. The corresponding previously saved scenario-cluster
95\% intervals are $[-0.167,-0.015]$ and $[-0.157,-0.039]$.
These are unadjusted per-comparison intervals. Thus, lower input relative
to Rule and Fixed Structured should not be interpreted as establishing
that learned routing is preferable to omitting long-term resources.

% Results: mechanism analysis, not a claim of causal quality improvement.
\paragraph{Which decision stages affect the selected action?}
An offline replay exactly reproduces PM and Rule actions on all 204 external
states. All states have 16 legal actions. No highest-score tie occurs, so
cost tie-breaking changes no decision. Misuse filtering removes candidates
in 200 states but never changes the final choice relative to unconstrained
response-score maximization. Omission and strategy thresholds exclude no
actions, and no fallback is used. On this external sample, the chosen action
is therefore identical to the response ranker's unconstrained argmax;
resource savings cannot be credited to an empirically active cost tie-break.
In a separate replay of 1,728 out-of-fold development predictions using the
final consensus thresholds, misuse filtering changes 97 choices, while
cost tie-breaking again changes none. This replay characterizes decisions
and does not measure causal changes in response quality.

\paragraph{Strategy preference and transfer.}
Among 5,568 development response comparisons that hold the memory
configuration fixed and swap RS/R0, the saved dual-order labels favor RS
in 2,673 cases and R0 in 1,313, with 1,582 ties. Out-of-fold predictions
rank RS higher in 4,147 of these action pairs. On external states, RS is
ranked higher in 1,536/1,632 matched-memory action pairs, and the selected
RS rate is 192/204. This directly describes the response-ranking behavior;
the later strategy-quality tie-break is not needed to explain these
external choices. The separately scored ESConv diagnostic uses GPT-4o
and favors R0 more often. These differences motivate a transfer/calibration
limitation, but do not identify judge family as the cause.

% Results: archived cost-matched evidence, separate from the original main table.
\paragraph{Cost-matched fixed routing.}
ME+R0 was chosen using development validation under a mean cost proxy no
greater than PM's, not by searching external response scores. Matching is
an average-budget criterion rather than exact per-turn equality. The
archived external diagnostic reports mean inputs of 1,294.6 for PM and
1,283.8 for ME+R0, and PM-minus-ME+R0 Emotional Support of $-0.172$ under a
separate two-candidate GPT-4o protocol. An archived 40-state, 80-call
forced-swap probe reports a two-order-average difference of $-0.150$
with a unit-bootstrap interval of $[-0.250,-0.050]$.
These absolute scores are not merged into the six-candidate table.
The archived report and available code were recovered; their referenced
raw score files were not available for independent recomputation here.
The evidence supports reporting a competitive simple fixed policy,
not a demonstrated external advantage for learned routing.

% New rerun supplement: different generator execution and judge from old V4.
\paragraph{Resource savings and fixed-input seeds in the rerun.}
On the 204 rerun states with Filter OFF, PM uses 20.70\% fewer generator
input tokens than Rule and 22.03\% fewer than Fixed Structured.
The paired token differences are $-332.76$ (user-cluster 95\% CI
$[-385.09,-283.35]$) and $-360.14$ ($[-412.50,-310.45]$), respectively.
The input-saving direction is consistent across all three fixed seeker
seeds. These intervals describe the 204 scored states and should not be
attached to the original 1,020-turn population means. Seed-specific quality
differences vary, and candidate position is confounded with seed and turn
in the original rerun ordering; the seed check therefore does not establish
quality robustness.

\paragraph{Candidate-order sensitivity in the rerun.}
We selected 48 states without consulting response outcomes: eight per
seeker-seed/turn stratum, covering all 18 users and 34 scenarios. We rescored
the same six completed replies in their original and exactly reversed
orders with GPT-4.1-mini-2025-04-14, retaining all other prompt fields and
serialization order. All 96 quality requests completed and were checked
against their uploaded requests and returned response identifiers.
For Filter OFF, PM-minus-Rule Overall changes from $+0.104$ to $-0.042$;
PM-minus-Fixed changes from $+0.146$ to $0.000$.
Their two-order averages are $+0.031$ (user-cluster 95\% CI
$[-0.061,+0.170]$) and $+0.073$ ($[-0.051,+0.225]$), respectively.
The point estimates are sensitive to candidate order. The averaged
differences remain small and uncertain; this is not a passed equivalence
test or validation of the original GPT-4o table's candidate order.

\paragraph{Exploratory demand for historical information.}
A response-blind judge inspection of all 204 rerun states used only
authorized history and the actual generator-visible context window,
excluding current-topic evaluator annotations. Its first outputs labeled
169 states as potentially helped by additional history and 35 as not
needing additional history; none was labeled as requiring an unavailable
historical fact to answer an explicit recall request. Twelve separate
synthetic annotation controls passed. However, 35 first outputs failed
verbatim historical-citation checks, and 25 remained unresolved after
the frozen retry limit. All original labels and failures are retained.
These are exploratory LLM annotations, not a validated recall benchmark
or evidence that historical information is unnecessary.
The existing separate omission scorer assesses missing useful authorized
context, including evaluator-only current-topic information; it should
not be relabeled as a pure cross-session memory-recall measure.

% Discussion: concise contribution boundary.
The evidence establishes a deployable pre-retrieval source allocator,
learnable allocation signal in controlled synthetic development, and
reduced input relative to specified high-resource structured policies.
It does not yet establish that this learned PM is preferable to simple
low-cost configurations, or that the resource reduction preserves
performance on a validated history-required task. Such a claim would
require an independently specified probe with verified needed facts,
retrievable evidence, and a scorer shown to distinguish omission.
